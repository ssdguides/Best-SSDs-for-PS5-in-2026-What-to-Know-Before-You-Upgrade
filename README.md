The PS5 doesn’t give you much room. The original console has 825GB of internal storage, and once the system takes its share you’re left with about 667GB. A handful of modern AAA games plus patches will fill that quickly.

An internal M.2 NVMe SSD solves this properly, because the console can run PS5 games directly from it. The catch is that the PS5 is picky about what goes in the expansion slot.

Sony’s requirements, simplified

A PCIe Gen4 x4 M.2 NVMe drive with an M-key connector. M.2 SATA isn’t supported.
Sony recommends at least 5,500MB/s sequential read. Nearly every Gen4 gaming SSD clears that, so treat it as a filter, not a ranking.
Total thickness including cooling can’t exceed 11.25mm, and Sony requires an effective heat-dissipation solution.
In practice, you’ll be buying a 2280 drive.

The warning many guides skip: HMB

Some SSDs carry their own DRAM chip to track where your data lives. Others, called HMB drives, borrow a slice of the host device’s memory to save money. Sony says the PS5 doesn’t support HMB, and that drives relying on it may be slower than expected. We haven’t found a Sony measurement of the slowdown, so we don’t quote a number. Our approach: prefer a drive with onboard DRAM when prices are close, and if you consider an HMB drive like the Lexar NM790, read independent PS5 test results for your exact capacity first.

The drives worth comparing

Samsung 990 PRO with Heatsink: DRAM, PS5-compatible heatsink, up to 4TB.
WD_BLACK SN850X: DRAM and the widest capacity range, up to 8TB. Make sure you’re buying a heatsink SKU.
WD_BLACK SN850P: officially licensed for PS5, with a heatsink built for the slot.
Kingston FURY Renegade: DRAM and a five-year warranty. Price it against the others.
Lexar NM790: budget-friendly, but an HMB design.

How much storage do you need?

Add up the install sizes of the games you play every month, then add 40 to 50 percent for patches, new releases and captures. Round up to the next common capacity. If that lands between 1TB and 2TB, compare the price per terabyte of both before deciding.

One more mistake to avoid

External USB drives can store PS5 games but can’t run them, so you’d have to copy games back to play. If you want more playable storage, you need an internal M.2 drive.
